## What Is Mexican Citizenship by Naturalization?

Mexican citizenship by naturalization grants full legal citizenship to foreign nationals who have resided legally in Mexico for the required period. As a Mexican citizen you receive:

- A Mexican passport (visa-free access to 150+ countries)
- The right to vote and stand for public office (with exceptions)
- Full protection under Mexican law
- The ability to pass citizenship to your children
- Unrestricted right to live, work, and own property in Mexico

**Mexico recognizes dual citizenship** — you generally do not need to renounce your current citizenship when naturalizing. (Verify with your home country's rules.)

## Eligibility Requirements

### Standard Pathway — 5 Years Legal Residency
Most foreign nationals who have lived legally in Mexico (on Temporary or Permanent Residency) for at least 5 consecutive years qualify.

Requirements:
- 5 years of legal residency (typically 4 years Temporary + 1 year Permanent)
- Demonstrable ties to Mexico (employment, property, family, or community involvement)
- Basic Spanish language proficiency
- Knowledge of Mexican history and culture (civics test)
- Clean criminal record

### Accelerated Pathway — 2 Years (Reduced Requirement)
Eligibility reduced to **2 years** for:
- Spouses or common-law partners of Mexican nationals
- Parents of Mexican-born children
- Nationals of Latin American countries or Spain

### Special Cases
- Minor children of naturalizing parents may naturalize simultaneously
- Political asylum or refugee status holders have separate provisions

## The Naturalization Process

### Step 1: Eligibility Assessment (Free)
MexVisa Pro reviews your residency history, legal status, and documentation to confirm eligibility before filing.

### Step 2: Document Collection
- Valid passport
- Permanent Residency card (current, not expired)
- Birth certificate (apostilled and translated into Spanish)
- Marriage certificate if applicable (apostilled)
- Proof of residency in Mexico (utility bills, rental contracts, bank statements — 5 years)
- RFC (Mexican tax ID)
- CURP (Mexican population registry number)
- Criminal background checks (from home country and Mexico)
- Two character references from Mexican nationals

### Step 3: Spanish & Civics Exam
Applicants must demonstrate:
- **Spanish language** — basic conversational ability (written exam at SRE office)
- **Mexican history and values** — basic civics knowledge test

MexVisa Pro provides preparation materials and practice questions for both exams.

### Step 4: SRE Application Filing
Your complete file is submitted to the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores (SRE). The government review period is typically 4–8 months.

### Step 5: Oath Ceremony (Carta de Naturalización)
Upon approval, you are invited to an oath-of-allegiance ceremony at the SRE office. Your Carta de Naturalización (naturalization certificate) is issued at this event.

### Step 6: Mexican Passport Application
With your Carta de Naturalización, you can immediately apply for a Mexican passport at any SRE delegación office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does Mexican citizenship take?**
The full process typically takes 6–12 months from filing to oath ceremony. Government review at SRE is the longest phase (4–8 months). Document collection and preparation takes 4–8 weeks with MexVisa Pro's guidance.

**Do I have to give up my current citizenship?**
Mexico allows dual citizenship — you do not need to renounce your original nationality to become Mexican. However, some countries (e.g., certain European nations, India) do not allow their citizens to hold dual nationality. Check your home country's rules before proceeding.

**Is the Spanish test difficult?**
The Spanish language exam tests basic conversational and written ability — not advanced fluency. Most applicants who have lived in Mexico for 5 years pass without formal study. MexVisa Pro provides practice materials.

**What is the civics test about?**
The civics exam covers Mexican history (pre-colonial, colonial, independence, Revolution), the Mexican Constitution, and national symbols. It is a written multiple-choice test. MexVisa Pro provides a full preparation guide.

**Can my children become Mexican citizens at the same time?**
Yes. Minor children of a naturalizing parent may apply for citizenship simultaneously or derive citizenship from the parent's naturalization. Additional documentation for each child is required.
