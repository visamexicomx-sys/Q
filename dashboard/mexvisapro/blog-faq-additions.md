## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much income do I need for Mexico temporary residency in 2026?**
For 2026, Mexico temporary residency requires proof of approximately $1,620 USD per month in regular income (salary, pension, rental income, or remote work earnings) OR approximately $27,000 USD in savings or investment accounts. INM updates these figures annually based on Mexico's UMA (daily minimum wage unit). MexVisa Pro confirms current requirements at your free consultation.

**How long does it take to get temporary residency in Mexico?**
MexVisa Pro processes temporary residency applications in the Riviera Maya in as few as 4 business days from the day complete documents are submitted. The full timeline, from initial consultation to card in hand, is typically 1–2 weeks. Consulate applications (for those outside Mexico) take 2–6 weeks depending on appointment availability.

**Can I apply for temporary residency while already in Mexico?**
Yes. If you entered Mexico on a valid tourist permit (FMM), you can apply for temporary residency directly at an INM office in Mexico without returning to your home country. This is the most common route for clients of MexVisa Pro in Playa del Carmen, Tulum, and Cancún.

**Can I work with Mexico temporary residency?**
Temporary residency alone does not authorize you to work for a Mexican employer. You must add a work permit endorsement (permiso de trabajo) to your residency card. Remote workers earning income from foreign employers do not need a separate work permit. MexVisa Pro can add work authorization simultaneously with your residency application.

**What is the difference between temporary and permanent residency in Mexico?**
Temporary residency (Residente Temporal) is valid for 1–4 years and must be renewed annually. Permanent residency (Residente Permanente) is indefinite and never requires renewal. After 4 years of temporary residency, you qualify for permanent residency. Permanent residency also includes automatic work authorization.

**How much does Mexico immigration consulting cost at MexVisa Pro?**
MexVisa Pro's temporary residency consulting starts at $500 USD. Government INM fees are additional (~$130–$160 USD). The initial consultation is free. All fees are quoted upfront with no hidden charges.

**What documents do I need for Mexico temporary residency?**
Core documents include: valid passport (6+ months validity), proof of income or savings (3–6 months bank statements), passport photos, and a completed INM application form. If applying from abroad, you also need proof of address in Mexico or a consulate appointment. MexVisa Pro provides a personalized document checklist.

**Can I renew Mexico temporary residency?**
Yes. Temporary residency is renewed annually for up to 4 years. MexVisa Pro handles renewal applications, which are simpler than the initial application. After 4 years, you may apply for permanent residency instead of renewing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who qualifies directly for Mexico permanent residency without 4 years of temporary residency?**
You can qualify directly for permanent residency if you are: (1) age 60+ with sufficient retirement income or assets (~$2,700 USD/month or ~$108,000 USD in savings); (2) a spouse, minor child, or parent of a Mexican national; (3) a high-income or high-asset investor. Most other applicants must hold temporary residency for 4 consecutive years first.

**How long does Mexico permanent residency take to process?**
With MexVisa Pro, the full process from document submission to card issuance takes 3–6 weeks. Government processing at INM is the primary variable. Applicants upgrading from temporary residency generally process faster than new applicants.

**Does Mexico permanent residency expire?**
Mexico permanent residency does not expire — it is indefinite. The physical residency card is valid for 10 years and must be renewed as a card (not as a status) when it expires. Your legal right to reside in Mexico is permanent.

**Can I work with permanent residency in Mexico?**
Yes. Unlike temporary residency, permanent residency includes full and unrestricted work authorization without any additional permit. You may work for any Mexican employer, be self-employed, or operate a business.

**How long after permanent residency can I apply for Mexican citizenship?**
After holding permanent residency for 2 years — and having accumulated a total of 5 years of legal residency in Mexico — you may apply for naturalization. Spouses of Mexican nationals can apply for citizenship after just 2 years of legal residency, regardless of status.

**What income do I need for permanent residency in Mexico?**
For the direct retirement pathway (age 60+), you need approximately $2,700 USD/month in pension or investment income OR approximately $108,000 USD in liquid assets (2026 INM rates). For those upgrading from 4 years of temporary residency, no new income proof is required beyond demonstrating continued legal status.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do digital nomads working remotely need a Mexico work permit?**
No. If you work remotely for a foreign employer and receive income from outside Mexico, you do not need a Mexican work permit. Temporary residency is sufficient to live legally in Mexico as a remote worker. A work permit is only required when working for a Mexican employer or invoicing Mexican clients in Mexico.

**Can I get a Mexico work permit without residency?**
No — in Mexico, work authorization is tied to your residency status. You must hold or simultaneously apply for Temporary Residency before a work permit can be added. MexVisa Pro files both applications together to minimize total wait time.

**How long does a Mexico work permit last?**
Work permits endorsed on Temporary Residency cards expire with the residency card and are renewed annually. If you hold Permanent Residency, work authorization is indefinite and requires no separate renewal.

**Can I be self-employed with a Mexico work permit?**
Yes. MexVisa Pro can file for a self-employment work permit covering independent professional activity. This allows you to invoice Mexican clients, register as a freelancer with SAT, and operate legally as a sole trader in Mexico.

**What is the difference between a work permit and a business visa in Mexico?**
These are not separate document types — both refer to work authorization added to your residency status. "Business visa" typically describes the investor/entrepreneur pathway to residency. A work permit is the specific endorsement on your residency card authorizing employment activity.

**Can my employer sponsor my Mexico work permit?**
Yes. Mexican employers can sponsor work permit applications for foreign employees. The employer must provide their company registration documents (RFC, IMSS) and an employment contract. MexVisa Pro coordinates between you and your employer throughout the process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Mexico have an official digital nomad visa?**
No — Mexico does not offer a formal "digital nomad visa" as of 2026. Remote workers who want to live legally in Mexico for more than 180 days should apply for Temporary Residency (Residente Temporal), which allows you to live in Mexico for 1–4 years while working remotely for foreign employers.

**Can I work remotely in Mexico without a work permit?**
Yes. If you work for a foreign employer and receive income from outside Mexico, you do not need a Mexican work permit. Temporary Residency is sufficient to live legally in Mexico as a digital nomad. A Mexican work permit is only needed if you work for a Mexican company or invoice Mexican clients.

**How long can I stay in Mexico as a digital nomad?**
On a tourist permit (FMM), you can stay up to 180 days. For longer stays, you need Temporary Residency (1–4 years, renewable). MexVisa Pro processes Temporary Residency in as little as 4 business days from $500 USD.

**Do I pay taxes in Mexico as a digital nomad?**
You may have Mexican tax obligations if you spend 183+ days per calendar year in Mexico (you become a Mexican tax resident). Mexico has tax treaties with many countries to prevent double taxation. MexVisa Pro recommends consulting a Mexican accountant (contador) for your specific situation — we can refer qualified tax professionals in Riviera Maya.

**What is the best city in Riviera Maya for digital nomads?**
Playa del Carmen is the most popular — centrally located, affordable coworking spaces, strong expat community, excellent internet infrastructure. Tulum attracts wellness-focused nomads and is growing fast. Cancún has the best transport links. All three are served by MexVisa Pro for immigration needs.

**Can I bring my family to Mexico as a digital nomad?**
Yes. Once you have Temporary Residency, your spouse and children can apply for Family Reunification Residency, granting them the same legal status. MexVisa Pro handles the full family application from $500 USD per family member.

**How much does temporary residency cost for digital nomads in Mexico?**
MexVisa Pro's consulting fee starts at $500 USD. Government INM fees are approximately $130–$160 USD. Total cost is typically $650–$700 USD. The initial consultation is free.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long do I need to live in Mexico before applying for citizenship?**
The standard requirement is 5 years of legal residency in Mexico (typically 4 years Temporary Residency + 1 year Permanent Residency). The requirement is reduced to 2 years for spouses of Mexican nationals or citizens of Latin American countries and Spain.

**Do I have to give up my current citizenship to become Mexican?**
No. Mexico recognizes dual citizenship — you do not need to renounce your original nationality when naturalizing as a Mexican citizen. However, some countries do not allow their citizens to hold dual nationality. Check your home country's rules before beginning the process.

**Is there a Spanish language test for Mexican citizenship?**
Yes. Applicants must pass a Spanish language proficiency test administered at the SRE (Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores) office. The test covers basic conversational and written Spanish. Most applicants who have lived in Mexico for 5+ years pass without formal preparation. MexVisa Pro provides practice materials.

**Is there a civics test for Mexican citizenship?**
Yes. Alongside the Spanish test, applicants must demonstrate basic knowledge of Mexican history, the Mexican Constitution, and national values. MexVisa Pro provides a complete preparation guide covering all tested topics.

**How much does Mexican citizenship cost?**
MexVisa Pro's consulting fee starts at $1,500 USD. The SRE government filing fee is approximately $100 USD. Document apostille and translation costs vary by country. Total estimated cost is $1,700–$2,000 USD.

**Can my children become Mexican citizens at the same time?**
Yes. Minor children of a naturalizing parent may apply simultaneously. Each child requires separate documentation and a small additional government fee. MexVisa Pro coordinates multi-applicant family files.

**What Mexican passport access do I get with citizenship?**
A Mexican passport grants visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to over 150 countries, including the Schengen Area, the United Kingdom, Japan, and most of Latin America. It is a strong travel document for internationally mobile individuals.
